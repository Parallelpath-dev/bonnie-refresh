02

Casual gardener interviews

We sat down with ten casual gardeners to hear how they started, where they go for help and what goes wrong. They love the idea of a garden, and every one of them has lost a plant. Most couldn’t say why.

Meet Catherine.

“Hi, I’m Cat.”

“Would you call yourself a gardener?”
“Yes.”
“Why?”

“Because I have had a garden, and I try to have a garden. Um, as often as I can.”

“My mom’s side of the family are all gardeners, so every time I would visit my grandma, she would instantly take us from the car to her garden.”

“So I always wanted to do it. I was always around it.”

“I liked the idea of having everything from scratch, and from something that you grew outside.”

Catherine, casual gardener

They want to grow. They’re afraid it’ll die.

All ten told us about a plant that didn’t make it. Most now ask AI before they ask a person, and starter plants feel like a safer bet than seed.

The ten people we heard from.

Main takeaways.

The patterns that kept coming up across our ten conversations.

Everyone has lost a plant.

10 of 10 told a failure story. 6 of 10 couldn’t say what went wrong.

The hard part starts after planting.

9 of 10 struggle with care, not choosing or planting. Watering comes up most.

AI is the first stop.

6 of 10 ask ChatGPT or Claude before anyone else. 4 of 10 go with whatever it says.

Gardening runs in the family.

8 of 10 learned from family, usually a parent or grandparent.

Seed isn’t about saving money.

0 of 10 chose seed because it’s cheaper. They grow from seed for the fun, the pride or the tradition.

No one named a plant brand.

0 of 10 mentioned Bonnie or any plant brand on their own.

03

Mystery shopping

We sent our team into stores the way a casual gardener would, with one goal: grow some food, after a history of killing plants. Here’s what they found on the shelf and in the aisle.

We shopped like beginners.

Our team visited big box stores, local garden centers and feed stores in eight markets in late October and early November. Each visit followed the same brief: ask for help, compare what’s there and note what a beginner would see.

8

markets across 7 states

22

store visits

1,772

miles between our farthest stops, Somerville, MA, and Boulder, CO

What our shoppers saw.

What stood out.

The patterns that showed up from store to store.

Season decides the shelf. Bonnie was there at 3 of 6 planting-season stops and 0 of 13 off-season.

At 8 of 22 stops, there was no one to ask about plants.

Every time an associate named Bonnie, it was as the store’s supplier: “well all we sell is Bonnie!”

Harvest Select and Foodie Fresh showed up at 0 of 6 stops where they’re sold.

Bonnie’s local-growing story showed up at 0 of 17 stops that reported on it.

04

Search and AI audit

We asked Google and five AI tools 68 questions beginners actually ask, from “where do I even start” to “why do my plants keep dying,” and recorded everything that came back: the answers, the sources they lean on and the brands they name.

We asked like beginners.

Every question was written the way a new gardener types or talks, then run through Google and five AI chatbots in early November.

6

AI platforms: Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode, ChatGPT, Meta AI, Gemini and Perplexity

beginner questions

Brands show up only at the shelf.

Until people shop, the answers come from other gardeners, not brands. Bonnie was named 13 times across Google’s AI Overviews and five chatbots, every time on a where-to-buy or brand question. No brand owns the learning, the seed-or-plants choice or the moment a plant dies. On all 5 questions about plants struggling or dying, Google’s AI Overviews cite Reddit or YouTube.

AI sends beginners to the local nursery.

Asked where to buy vegetable plants, all five chatbots picked the local nursery. Big box comes across as the cheap, hit-or-miss option, and that’s where Bonnie gets placed. ChatGPT’s answers were localized to Austin.

The plant is the same. The care isn’t.

One answer drew the line our shoppers saw in the aisle: healthy Bonnie plants at one store, dry ones at another.

Meta AI, asked if Home Depot and Lowe’s plants are any good

“Home Depot and Lowe’s mostly sell the same big national growers (Bonnie Plants is the main one) that a lot of nurseries also carry… The difference is what happens after it arrives at the store.”

AI makes Bonnie’s case, without Bonnie.

Asked whether starter plants are easier for someone who’s killed plants before, 3 of 4 platforms said yes. None named Bonnie.

Perplexity, asked: I’ve killed plants before. Would buying starter plants be easier than doing seeds?

“Yes—starter plants are usually easier, especially if you’ve struggled with plants before. They’ve already germinated and grown for weeks, so you skip the fussy stages of sprouting, thinning, repotting, and hardening off.”

Ask about Bonnie by name, and AI cites the skeptics.
